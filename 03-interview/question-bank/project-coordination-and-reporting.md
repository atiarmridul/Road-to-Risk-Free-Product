# Project Coordination and QA Reporting Questions

> **সহজভাবে / In simple words:** These questions test how you organize work, communicate risk, and help a team make decisions.
>
> **কেন গুরুত্বপূর্ণ / Why it matters:** Senior QA roles require clear coordination and reporting, not only test execution.

## Which test-management tools have you used?

Name only tools you have genuinely used. Explain what you did with them: design cases, organize suites, connect requirements, record execution, report results, or support release decisions. Tool names are weaker than a concrete workflow.

## A developer rejects your defect. What do you do?

1. Recheck requirements, acceptance criteria, and business rules.
2. Reproduce from a clean state.
3. Improve the evidence: build, environment, data, steps, logs, request/response, screenshot, or video.
4. Explain expected versus actual behavior and user impact.
5. Discuss the facts directly with the developer.
6. Ask the product owner or business analyst to resolve requirement ambiguity.
7. Record the final decision and any accepted risk.

The goal is shared understanding, not “winning” an argument.

## How do you estimate testing work?

Break the work into requirement review, test design, data and environment setup, execution, defect investigation, retesting, regression, reporting, and coordination. Adjust for complexity, dependencies, uncertainty, team capacity, and historical evidence. State assumptions instead of presenting a single unexplained number.

## How do you plan team capacity?

- Start with actual availability, holidays, and support commitments.
- Map skills and ownership to the highest-risk work.
- Include time for reviews, defect investigation, and unexpected retesting.
- Keep a visible buffer for uncertainty.
- Compare planned and actual effort to improve future estimates.

## How do you monitor people who report to you?

Use clear outcomes, regular check-ins, visible blockers, review quality, and coaching. Avoid measuring productivity only by ticket or test-case count. Discuss growth goals privately and recognize contributions publicly when appropriate.

## How do you resolve conflict?

Clarify the facts and the shared goal, listen to each perspective, identify the real disagreement, compare options and risks, agree on ownership, and follow up. Keep the discussion about the work rather than the person.

## How do you link defects to requirements?

Link the defect to its story or requirement and, where useful, to the relevant test and build. A traceability matrix can help regulated or complex projects, but direct tool links are often more maintainable for normal Agile work.

## What belongs in a defect report?

- Clear title and identifier
- Product version or build
- Environment and relevant configuration
- Preconditions and test data
- Minimal reproduction steps
- Expected and actual results
- Frequency and impact
- Severity and suggested priority context
- Evidence such as logs, screenshots, videos, or API traffic
- Links to requirement, story, test, and related incidents

## What reports might QA prepare?

- Test execution or cycle report
- Defect and triage summary
- Daily or sprint quality status
- Regression report
- Release-readiness recommendation
- Test-closure report
- Production smoke or post-release report
- Incident or root-cause review contribution

A useful report explains status, evidence, remaining risk, blockers, owners, and the decision required.
